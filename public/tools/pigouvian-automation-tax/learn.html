The AI Layoff Trap & Pigouvian Automation Tax

Formalizing the Macroeconomic Prisoner's Dilemma of Enterprise AI Adoption, Aggregate Demand Destruction, and Optimal Pigouvian Speed Governors.

Research Grounding: Based on the 2026 paper "The AI Layoff Trap" by Brett Hemenway Falk (University of Pennsylvania, Department of Computer and Information Science) and Gerry Tsoukalas (Boston University Questrom School of Business / Wharton).

1. The Economic Paradox: "Firing Your Own Customers"

When an individual enterprise automates a white-collar or operational workflow using Large Language Models or autonomous agents, it captures 100% of the cost savings (substituting human wages $w$ with compute cost $c_{AI} \ll w$). However, the worker who is displaced experiences a collapse in income. Because workers have a high Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC ≈ 0.85), their loss of wages diminishes aggregate purchasing power across the economy.

An individual firm only sees a tiny fraction ($1/N$) of that demand loss directly, treating the broader destruction of purchasing power as an externalized cost. Yet, when every competing firm pursues the same cost-cutting strategy, the entire consumer base suffers a severe reduction in purchasing power, causing corporate sales to plunge.

2. The Game-Theoretic Prisoner's Dilemma

Why can't market competition solve this on its own? Because of the dominant strategy trap:

  • If Competitors Automate: A firm that retains human labor has higher operating expenses ($w > c_{AI}$), gets priced out of the market, and goes bankrupt.
  • If Competitors Retain Humans: An individual firm that automates captures supernormal short-term profit margins.

Therefore, regardless of what competitors do, full automation is the strictly dominant individual strategy. All firms defect, resulting in the Nash equilibrium where aggregate demand collapses—even though every firm would have been more profitable under the cooperative, human-complementary optimum.

3. Why Conventional Interventions Fail

FAILURE ANALYSIS

1. Workforce Retraining & Upskilling

The capability frontier of AI expands continuously. Workers retrained for 6–18 months emerge to find that the new tasks they learned have already been automated by next-generation foundation models.

FAILURE ANALYSIS

2. Universal Basic Income (UBI) & Capital Taxes

UBI redistributes purchasing power after the fact, but does not alter the firm's marginal cost comparison ($c_{AI} < w$). Thus, firms continue to accelerate human displacement, exacerbating the structural deficit.

4. The Solution: Pigouvian Automation Tax (τ*)

Falk & Tsoukalas prove that the only micro-theoretically sound remedy is a Pigouvian tax levied directly at the margin of automated labor substitution:

τ* = MPC × (w - c_AI) × (1 - 1/N) Where: w = Human wage rate ($/hr) c_AI = Marginal compute execution cost ($/hr equivalent) MPC = Displaced worker marginal propensity to consume N = Effective competing firms in market segment

The Pigouvian tax acts as a macroeconomic speed governor: it forces firms to internalize the demand-destruction cost, ensuring that automation occurs only when AI's productivity gains genuinely exceed the social displacement cost.
